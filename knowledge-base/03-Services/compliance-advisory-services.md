# Compliance Advisory Services

This article describes the Compliance Advisory Services from Encryption Consulting (EC). EC checks how encryption, key management, and PKI controls meet regulatory and industry requirements and gives a clear plan to close the gaps. It is for compliance, risk, audit, and security teams preparing for an assessment or responding to findings.

## What the service covers

The service focuses on the cryptographic part of compliance. Activities include:

- **Policy review:** review of cryptographic, key management, and certificate policies against requirements.
- **Infrastructure assessment:** review of encryption systems, Hardware Security Modules (HSMs), key managers, and Public Key Infrastructure (PKI).
- **Questionnaires:** tailored questions to capture current practice across teams.
- **Gap identification:** mapping of each requirement to current controls and evidence.
- **Findings and recommendations:** prioritized actions with owners.
- **Remediation and compliance roadmaps:** step-by-step plans with milestones.
- **Ongoing advisory (optional):** watching for regulatory change and running periodic reassessments.

Frameworks commonly in scope:

| Framework | Cryptography focus examples |
|---|---|
| Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) v4.0.1 | Protection of stored account data (Requirement 3), strong cryptography in transit (Requirement 4), cipher suite and protocol inventory (Requirement 12.3.3) |
| NIST Special Publication (SP) 800-57 and NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) | Key management lifecycle, key lengths, cryptoperiods |
| Federal Information Processing Standard (FIPS) 140-3 | Use of validated cryptographic modules, FIPS 140-2 to 140-3 transition |
| General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) | Encryption as a protection measure for personal data |
| Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) | Encryption of electronic protected health information |
| Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) | Cryptographic controls and key management for financial entities in the EU |
| Network and Information Security Directive (NIS2) | Policies on cryptography and encryption for essential and important entities |
| electronic IDentification, Authentication and trust Services (eIDAS) | Trust services, qualified certificates, and signatures |
| SOC 2 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 | Cryptographic control objectives and evidence |

> **Note:** EC provides advisory support. A formal audit opinion or certification is issued by the qualified assessor or certification body, not by the advisory engagement.

## Who it is for

- Organizations preparing for a PCI DSS, SOC 2, or ISO 27001 assessment.
- Financial entities in scope for DORA and organizations in scope for NIS2.
- Teams that received audit findings related to encryption, keys, or certificates.
- Organizations planning the move from FIPS 140-2 to FIPS 140-3 validated modules.

## Engagement phases

| Phase | Main activities | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery and data gathering | Confirm frameworks and scope. Collect policies and evidence. Send questionnaires. | Scope and evidence list |
| 2. Current state analysis | Review policies and existing encryption capabilities. Interview control owners. | Current state summary |
| 3. Gap assessment | Map each requirement to controls and evidence. Rate each gap. | Gap assessment report and risk register |
| 4. Findings and recommendations | Write prioritized, practical fixes. | Findings and recommendations report |
| 5. Roadmap development | Order fixes with timelines and resource estimates. | Remediation and compliance roadmap |
| 6. Ongoing advisory (optional) | Monitor regulatory change and reassess on a schedule. | Periodic reassessment reports |

## Deliverables

- Compliance gap assessment report
- Requirement-to-control mapping
- Risk register
- Prioritized findings and recommendations report
- Remediation roadmap with milestones
- Strategy document for sustained compliance
- Audit-ready documentation package, such as updated policies and evidence index

## What EC needs from the customer

**Stakeholders**

- Compliance or risk lead and an executive sponsor.
- Control owners for encryption, key management, PKI, identity, and cloud.
- Internal audit contact, if available.

**Documents**

- In-scope frameworks and the last assessment report.
- Security, encryption, key management, and certificate policies.
- Data flow diagrams and scope definitions, such as the cardholder data environment.
- Evidence already collected for past audits.

**Access**

- Interview time with control owners.
- Read access to evidence repositories, when agreed.

## Typical timeline

Duration depends on the number of frameworks and business units. EC confirms the timeline during scoping.
